Mold is the most common household pollutant case of all — and the most commonly mishandled. Wiping away the black spots treats the symptom. Mold grows wherever **moisture** persists. Without finding the moisture source, it always comes back.

## Why mold is a health topic

Molds release spores and metabolic products into indoor air. According to the EPA and WHO, they can promote **respiratory irritation, allergies, and asthma** — especially in children, allergy sufferers, and people with weakened immune systems. Panic is still misplaced: what matters is the extent and duration of growth, not a single spot on the shower caulk.

## The three usual suspects

1. **Humidity management:** warm air holds a lot of water. When it cools against cold surfaces, moisture condenses — ideal growing conditions. Classic case: the cool bedroom with the door open to a warm living area, or an oversized AC that cools without dehumidifying.
2. **Thermal bridges:** poorly insulated corners, window reveals, and closets on exterior walls run colder than the rest — moisture settles there first.
3. **Building defects:** roof leaks, plumbing failures, rising damp, flooded basements. No amount of ventilation fixes these — they require repair.

## DIY or call the pros?

- **Small areas — the EPA's guideline is about 10 square feet (roughly 1 m²)** on hard surfaces: you can clean them yourself with detergent and water, gloves on, room ventilated. Experts generally advise against fogging living spaces with harsh biocide sprays.
- **Larger or recurring growth**, mold behind furniture, in carpets, inside walls, or after water damage belongs to professionals — including a cause analysis. Ballpark: a professional mold assessment with a written report runs a few hundred dollars.
- **Renters** should document the growth (photos, dates) and notify the landlord — determining the cause is the owner's job.

## Prevention in one sentence

**Keep indoor humidity between 40 and 60 percent:** ventilate thoroughly, run bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans, dehumidify damp basements, keep furniture a couple of inches off exterior walls — and watch the numbers with a hygrometer.
