Flint, Michigan made it painfully clear: the utility's water can meet every standard — and still **pick up lead in the last stretch into and inside your house**. The sources are lead service lines, lead solder (legal in the U.S. until 1986), and older brass fixtures.

## Why lead is taken so seriously

Lead accumulates in the body and, even in small amounts, harms **blood formation and the developing nervous system**. Unborn children, infants, and toddlers are most vulnerable — the EPA and CDC agree there is **no known safe level of lead exposure for children**. For adults the risk is lower, but chronic exposure remains pointless.

## The rules are tightening

The EPA's **Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (finalized 2024)** require water systems to **replace virtually all lead service lines within 10 years**, lower the action level from 15 to **10 µg/L**, and inventory every service line — you can ask your utility what your line is made of, and in many cities look it up on a public map. Europe is on the same path: the EU cuts its lead limit to 5 µg/L by 2036. In short: **lead pipes are on their way out everywhere — but millions are still in the ground.**

## Spotting lead plumbing

- **Year built:** U.S. homes before 1986 may have lead solder; service lines in pre-1960s neighborhoods are the prime suspects.
- **The scratch test:** exposed lead pipe is dull gray, soft (a key scratches it shiny), non-magnetic, and makes a dull thud when tapped — unlike magnetic steel or reddish copper.
- **When in doubt: measure.** A certified lab test for lead (see [Testing your tap water](/en/articles/testing-tap-water)) costs $20–60. Crucial: take a **first-draw sample** — water that sat in the pipes overnight.

## What helps immediately — and what actually solves it

**Immediate measures:** in the morning and after long absences, **run the tap until the water turns noticeably colder** (30 seconds to 2 minutes). Use only cold water for drinking, cooking, and formula — hot water dissolves more lead. An NSF/ANSI 53-certified filter (pitcher or faucet-mount, certified for lead) is a solid bridge solution.

**The real fix:** **replacing the lead line.** Under the new rules, utilities must replace their portion — ask about the schedule and about cost-sharing programs for the private portion. A documented reading above the action level is your strongest lever, whether you're an owner dealing with the utility or a renter dealing with a landlord.
