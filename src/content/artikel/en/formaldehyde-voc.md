Everyone knows it: the smell of new furniture, fresh paint, a new car. What you're smelling are **volatile organic compounds (VOCs)** — including the best-known troublemaker, **formaldehyde**, which can off-gas from glued wood products like particleboard and MDF.

## What's the problem?

Formaldehyde irritates eyes and airways even at low concentrations and is classified as a human carcinogen (nasopharynx, at high long-term exposures). Other VOCs — solvents from paints, lacquers, adhesives — cause headaches, fatigue, and mucous-membrane irritation in sensitive people. The typical pattern: **symptoms at home, relief on vacation.**

Context first: modern furniture and building materials are leagues better than the notorious particleboard of the 1970s and '80s — the era that gave the U.S. its FEMA-trailer and UFFI-insulation formaldehyde scandals. And the strongest off-gassing fades by itself within the **first weeks to months**.

## The classic sources

- **New furniture made of composite wood** (particleboard, MDF), especially large pieces like wardrobes and bed frames
- **Freshly renovated rooms:** paints, lacquers, adhesives, flooring
- **Older fixed installations from the '60s–'80s:** paneling and built-ins glued with formaldehyde resins
- **Laminate flooring and carpet adhesives**, depending on the product

## What actually helps

1. **Ventilate, ventilate, ventilate:** in the first weeks after buying furniture or renovating, air out thoroughly several times a day — by far the most effective lever.
2. **Shop by label:** in the U.S., composite wood must meet the **TSCA Title VI / CARB Phase 2** formaldehyde standard — look for the compliance label; choose low-VOC paints (Green Seal, GREENGUARD certified products).
3. **Let new furniture off-gas:** unpack immediately, ventilate the room intensively for the first days.
4. **For persistent symptoms: measure, don't guess.** An indoor-air test for formaldehyde and VOCs (lab-based kits from roughly $50–150, professional surveys from a few hundred) shows whether levels are actually elevated.
5. **Fix the source, don't mask it:** with contaminated older built-ins, only removal or proper sealing of the boards helps long-term — air fresheners just cover the problem.

If you spot suspects while house-hunting — say, wall-to-wall '70s paneling — put them straight onto the [pollutant checklist before signing](/en/articles/pollutants-home-buying).
